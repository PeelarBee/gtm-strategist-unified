# LinkedIn for B2B GTM

Source: curated from Maja Voje / GTM Strategist LinkedIn content. Use when a user wants to use LinkedIn for pipeline, personal brand, or GTM distribution — LinkedIn is treated here as a full GTM channel, not just a social network to post on.

## The 5-Second ICP Relevance Test (profile-as-landing-page)

Core distinction: "A resume answers 'here's my past.' A landing page answers 'here's what you get.'" A visitor decides relevance in about 5 seconds — audit or rebuild a profile top-to-bottom with that lens:

| Section | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Photo + Name | Clear, recognizable face, reads instantly | Blurry photos, sunglasses, low-quality/non-neutral background |
| Banner | Biggest visual real estate — lead with what's in it for the visitor. Pick one lane: (1) Branding, (2) Promo/offer, (3) Ecosystem | Mixing all three lanes at once |
| About | Not a CV — a scannable answer to "Are you relevant to me?" Optimize for keywords, one clear CTA, pin top skills | Reads like a biography — won't get indexed well by search/AI |
| Headline | Role + a real number + the keyword you want to rank for | Generic title with no proof or keyword |
| Activity | Keep it recent — even 2 recent posts reads as "alive" | A last post from 8+ months ago reads as inactive |
| Featured | Best visual inventory — pick a play: (1) offers → conversion, (2) best posts → social proof, (3) mix of both | Empty or outdated Featured section — wasted real estate |
| Experience | Optimize for logos + numbers people recognize — quantified wins, decks, presentation photos | Generic job descriptions with no proof |
| Skills + Recs | Reorder top skills to show first, ask colleagues to endorse/recommend | Ignoring — easy wins left on the table |

**5 shifts to prioritize:** (1) Banner leads with what they get; (2) Headline = role + real number + target keyword; (3) Featured = exact links you want demand flowing to, not vanity posts; (4) 2 recent posts minimum; (5) Experience packed with recognizable brands/numbers/media.

**5 technical fails to check for:** no website link on profile; CTA button stuck on "Connect" instead of "Follow" (Settings); About section reads like a biography; Featured section empty/dated; network still full of ex-colleagues with no ICP reach (skews what the algorithm serves you).

**Validation tip:** have the actual ICP do a usability pass on the profile — their read is the one that matters, not the profile owner's own opinion.

## 5 Modes of LinkedIn for GTM Strategy

A spectrum of how deeply to use LinkedIn, from foundational to insurance-policy. You don't need to activate all five at once — pick based on whether you're running an inbound motion, and how much team bandwidth exists.

| Mode | What it does | JTBD | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Indexed (The Foundation) | An optimized, findable profile | Shows up correctly the second someone Googles you or asks ChatGPT/Claude about you | LinkedIn, Canva, Claude, Figma |
| 2. Opinion Leader (The Engine) | Consistent content + value commenting | Builds trust/preference at scale, grows network, extractable ICP signal | Heyreach, Jungler, Lemlist, Notion |
| 3. GTM Engineer (The Converter) | Signal-based outbound, personalization at scale | Skips "content purgatory" — detects real signals (complaints, events, job changes) and converts them into landing outreach | Clay, Claude Code, Exa, Apify |
| 4. House of Brands (The Multiplier) | Multiple team profiles amplify one narrative | Team-wide lift, but a long game — turning experts into "creators" carries real opportunity cost | Dreamdata, Slack, Figma, Planable |
| 5. Media Machine (The Insurance) | New channels beyond LinkedIn (YouTube, Substack, X) | Hedges platform/algorithm risk — LinkedIn reach can be throttled or shifted at any time | YouTube, Substack, Beehiiv, X |

**Common trap:** obsessing over input metrics (5 posts/week, 40 DMs/day) instead of pipeline outcome — it's possible to hit 200K followers and 1M impressions with an empty pipeline. When a user reports on LinkedIn effort, ask about pipeline generated, not post count.

## 7 LinkedIn Playbooks (beginner → specialist progression)

Ordered from low-complexity/one-off to high-complexity/always-on. Most B2B GTM teams stop at playbook 1 or 4 and never progress — "Most B2B GTM teams run 1 LinkedIn play. They blast the same cold DM to 500 people."

1. **Personal network ICP** — export 1st-degree LinkedIn contacts, score with Clay, send a DM. *Tools: LinkedIn, CSV export, Clay.*
2. **ICP-relevant event play** — scrape an event's attendee/filter list, DM outreach (usually very high ROI given shared context). *Tools: Clay, LinkedIn, HeyReach, Apify.*
3. **ICP engagement play** — extract people who engaged with your posts and reach out to them specifically. *Tools: Clay, Jungler.*
4. **Cold DM campaign** — find ICPs on LinkedIn, send a DM/email campaign. *Tools: HeyReach, Clay, Instantly, LinkedIn Sales Navigator.*
5. **Signal-Pipeline Cold play** — run signal research with Claude Code + Exa, then personalize messages via Clay. *Tools: Exa, Claude Code, Clay, HeyReach.*
6. **Multichannel DMU play** — map the buying committee (see [frameworks.md](frameworks.md) DMU pyramid) and orchestrate ABM touchpoints across channels. *Tools: Exa, Clay.*
7. **LinkedIn Ads targeting** — identify activity triggers, automate ad campaigns around them. *Tools: LinkedIn Ads, Clay.*

When a user is doing playbook 1-2, they can likely get real ROI moving to 3-5 before needing the complexity of 6-7 — don't recommend the most advanced play if the foundational ones aren't running yet.
