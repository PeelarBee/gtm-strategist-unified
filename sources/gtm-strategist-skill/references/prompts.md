# 15 GTM Prompts for Claude

Source: curated from Maja Voje / GTM Strategist LinkedIn content ("15 GTM Prompts for Claude Opus"). These are templates — adapt the bracketed placeholders to the user's real specifics (their product, their segment, their actual call transcripts, their real numbers). Never fill in a placeholder with a guess; ask the user for the real input instead.

**Framing to keep in mind:** "The prompt gets you 60%. Your traction, your buyer's exact words, your dollar figures — that's the other 40%, and it's the part that makes the output yours." Don't just run these prompts verbatim — push the user for the specifics that make the output theirs, not generic.

The 15 prompts are organized across three stages of GTM maturity: **Cold Start** (figuring it out), **Heating Up** (saying it), **On Fire** (scaling it).

## Cold Start — "Figure It Out"

**1. ICP / buyer-hat prompt**
> Act as a B2B GTM strategist. Product: [one sentence]. Who I sell to: [segment]. Return the 3 segments worth attacking first — each with the trigger event that means "buy now."

**2. Market trend research**
> Research what changed in [category] over the last 90 days. Search aggressively, verify with 2+ sources. Return 5-7 shifts and the one I'm underreacting to.

**3. Call analysis**
> Read this sales call. [paste transcript]. Surface the 3 objections that mattered, in the buyer's words, and where the deal actually stalled. Then draft the follow-up that moves it forward.

**4. Positioning + differentiation**
> My alternatives: [2-4 competitor URLs]. My product: [what + who for]. Return one positioning statement, plus 3 differentiators — each tied to a real capability and naming the alternative it beats.

**5. Strategic narrative**
> Shape the proprietary truth only I can claim. What I believe my market doesn't yet: [contrarian insight]. Return the status quo I'm positioning against, plus a 120-word narrative.

## Heating Up — "Say It"

**6. Messaging + website teardown**
> Tear down this copy. [paste page]. My ICP: [who]. The job they hire me for: [JTBD]. Score how well it answers "is this for me?" and "will it work?" — then rewrite every weak line.

**7. Battle card**
> Build a battle card: [Product A] vs [Product B] for [ICP]. Rate both across [5-6 categories], one line of evidence each. Win only what I genuinely win — keep the real ties.

**8. Permissionless value-prop outbound**
> Write a cold email to [name, role, company]. What I already made for them, free: [the teardown / insight]. Lead with the value. The ask comes last, low-pressure. Under 90 words.

**9. Professional group post**
> Write a post for [the group] where [ICP] hangs out. The value I'm sharing: [insight / framework]. Teach it in full, end on a real question. No pitch, no link.

**10. Find relevant influencers + outreach**
> Find creators my ICP already follows. ICP: [describe]. Use web search. Return 8 names — and why my ICP trusts each. Then the outreach email that leads with what's in it for them.

## On Fire — "Scale It"

**11. Case study extraction**
> Turn this raw feedback into a case study. [paste notes]. Structure it before → during → after, opening on the result. Pull one quote in their exact words. If a number's missing, flag it — never invent one.

**12. Content scaling machine**
> I have one long-form piece: [describe it]. Turn it into a week of content — 5 assets, no two making the same point. Sequence them, strongest hook first.

**13. Sales deck opener**
> Write the opening 3 slides for [ICP]. What I sell: [one sentence]. The outcome: [number]. Open on their problem, then the cost of standing still, then the shift. No agenda slide, no company history.

**14. Loss analysis**
> Analyze why I lost this deal. [paste notes]. Tell me the real reason, not the stated one — and the exact moment it slipped. Then: what does the winner do that I don't? Be direct.

**15. Open a new market**
> I've won [current segment]. Find the next one. What works today: [use case + buyer]. Return 3 adjacent segments, ranked by how little I'd change to win. Name the trap.
