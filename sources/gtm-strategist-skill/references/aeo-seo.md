# AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

Source: curated from Maja Voje / GTM Strategist LinkedIn content, with George Chasiotis (AEO expert) and Ahrefs data. Use this whenever a user asks about showing up in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini answers, AI Overviews, or "why don't AI tools recommend us."

Context stat: "Only 14% of marketers feel confident in their AEO strategy. The other 86% are doing it anyway." — most companies genuinely don't know what AI says about them yet, which is why step 1 of the playbook below is always an audit, not a guess.

## The 6 Pillars

1. **Topic breadth and depth** — cover your category, then go deep, but stay close to your actual business model. Cautionary example: a company published 500+ off-topic review pages chasing volume and got crushed by a Google update. Don't recommend breadth for its own sake.
2. **Publishing velocity** — freshness compounds. AI assistants cite content that's roughly 26% fresher than what classic Google search cites — an old-but-authoritative page can still lose to a newer, thinner one.
3. **Content optimization** — LLMs read the top of the page first. Front-load the direct answer; focus beats length. Don't bury the answer under a long preamble.
4. **Crawl efficiency** — if AI bots can't reach your pages, nothing else matters. Most teams have never checked whether their `robots.txt` blocks AI crawlers — this is a 5-minute fix that's frequently skipped.
5. **Content monitoring** — fresh content ages fast. Apply a light refresh, a full update, or a full rewrite depending on how outdated/damaged the page's accuracy has become — don't treat all stale content the same way.
6. **Brand experience** — backlinks are now just one signal among many. Brands with the most web mentions (not just links) earn up to 10x more AI Overview mentions. Off-site presence matters as much as on-site SEO now.

## The AEO Playbook — 6 Steps

Run these roughly in order for a brand doing AEO for the first time:

1. **Audit your AI visibility** — run 15-25 real buyer prompts (the actual phrasing a buyer would type, not marketing language) across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. Check whether the brand is mentioned, recommended, and described accurately. Key insight: visibility is not uniform across engines — "you might be No.1 on Perplexity and invisible in ChatGPT," so audit each separately.
2. **Build a prompt library** — map the brand and every real competitor against core buying-journey prompts. This becomes a "Share of Model" tracker — treat it as a new pipeline metric to monitor over time, not a one-off check.
3. **Win late-funnel SEO first** — AI pulls from the same content that already ranks in classic search. Comparison pages, use-case pages, and technical/integration docs are wildly underrated for AEO — prioritize these over top-of-funnel blog content.
4. **Structure content for AI interpretability** — lead with direct answers, use semantic headings, add FAQ sections with schema markup. Stat: 83% of AI citations come from pages updated within the past 12 months — freshness is not optional, it's close to a hard requirement.
5. **Build citation signals off-site** — G2 reviews, LinkedIn thought leadership, Reddit presence, community forums, partner/integration directories. AI trusts consensus across the whole digital footprint, not just the company's own website.
6. **Monitor and iterate** — AEO is not "set it and forget it." Track Share of Model monthly, measure AI-referred traffic and its conversion rate *separately* from organic search traffic, and correct the narrative when an AI engine describes the brand inaccurately.

## Localization as an AEO/SEO lever

English is native to only 10-15% of the world. If a site isn't indexed in the languages its buyers actually speak, it doesn't exist to them when they ask an LLM for a vendor recommendation in their own language. When a user is only optimizing their English-language site, flag that non-English AEO is a largely untapped lever, not just a "nice to have" for international expansion.
